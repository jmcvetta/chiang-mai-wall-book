# Chiang Mai wall book: planning document

Status: initial plan, not a researched inventory or manuscript.

## Purpose

Produce a researched, illustrated e-book about the surviving wall and moatworks of Chiang Mai. The book follows the physical circuit, not a chronological survey of the city. Its central question is: **What is this particular piece of masonry, and what can we establish about its history?**

The book is the product. Publishing tools support it; this is not a software product. The goal is for the book to exist and be useful, whether or not its AI-generated prose qualifies for copyright protection.

Proposed working title: *Around the Wall: Chiang Mai, Section by Section*.

Proposed readership: curious residents, visitors, and readers of local history. Write in English, retain verified Thai place names alongside consistent English forms, and explain specialist terms. This readership and language are planning assumptions, not final publishing decisions.

## Editorial commitments

1. Start at the North Gate, at 12 o'clock in the reader's mental map, and proceed clockwise around the roughly square circuit until returning to the start.
2. Give each distinct surviving, period-specific section its own chapter. Two sections do not become one chapter merely because they touch, overlap, or blend together.
3. Include surviving moatworks: the stonework or other masonry that physically forms the moat. Apply the same section-and-period rule to these remains.
4. Do not give the body of water its own chapters or repetitive section-by-section treatment. Mention it only where necessary to locate or explain a physical structure.
5. Reference **every factual claim with an endnote**. This includes apparently obvious facts and facts in introductions, captions, maps, tables, timelines, and navigation text.
6. Prefer “we do not know,” a precisely stated research limit, or omission to an unsupported assertion. Neither narrative flow nor chapter symmetry justifies filling a gap.
7. Distinguish the history of a place from the age of the material standing there. A documented event at a gate does not date its present bricks.

This plan does not establish the number, boundaries, dates, or materials of surviving sections. Those are research outputs. Its route is an editorial structure, not a surveyed geometric description.

## Scope

### Include

- Extant wall masonry, including gate and corner structures on the circuit.
- Distinct construction, rebuilding, and restoration phases represented in surviving fabric, when evidence supports their identification.
- Surviving moat retaining walls, linings, and associated masonry that forms the moat, if identified. Survey both the city-side and outer-side banks; do not assume that both survive or are equally accessible.
- Interfaces between phases: abutments, overlaps, repairs, changes of construction, and uncertain transitions.
- Lost predecessors and wider historical events only when needed to explain an extant section, with the same evidence requirements as the rest of the book.
- Modern reconstruction where it forms part of what a reader sees. Do not exclude it because it is recent or describe it as ancient without evidence.

These are categories to investigate, not claims that examples of every category exist.

### Exclude

- A general history of Chiang Mai, an exhaustive account of its defenses, or a guide to unrelated attractions.
- Separate coverage of stretches of water, water quality, or a general hydrological history.
- Invented dates, builders, motives, battles, dimensions, vanished features, or construction sequences.
- Dating from appearance alone. Weathering, brick color, vegetation, and ruin do not by themselves establish age.
- AI-generated historical photographs or reconstructions used as evidence. Explanatory diagrams must distinguish documented features from uncertainty and cite their factual content.

A gap with no visible remains belongs in route continuity, not in an invented monument chapter. Record inaccessible or unexamined areas as survey limitations, not as proof of absence.

## Route and book organization

Use a north-up overview map and a single clockwise sequence:

| Route division | Direction of travel |
| --- | --- |
| Opening stop | North Gate and its identified fabric |
| North, eastern half | North Gate toward the northeast corner |
| East | Northeast corner toward the southeast corner |
| South | Southeast corner toward the southwest corner |
| West | Southwest corner toward the northwest corner |
| North, western half | Northwest corner back to North Gate |

These divisions organize the book; they do not determine chapter count. Verify the precise start point, place names, route geometry, and safe viewing positions before publishing them as facts.

Interleave wall and moatwork chapters by the position at which the reader encounters them. At the same position, cover the wall first, then city-side moatworks, then outer-side moatworks. Where several phases occupy the same footprint, place their chapters together and state that the reader remains at the same stop. Use the documented sequence where known; do not invent one to order chapters.

Treat the North Gate as one opening group. The closing approach links back to it rather than duplicating its chapters. Physical access may require a detour; reading order must not imply that a continuous, safe footpath exists.

### Provisional contents

- How to use the book: route, maps, endnotes, evidence limits, and survey date.
- A short orientation: only the sourced historical and architectural context needed to read the remains.
- Section chapters in clockwise order, grouped under the route divisions above.
- Closing synthesis: what the documented sections establish, and what remains unknown.
- Endnotes, grouped by chapter, at the back of the book.
- Bibliography, place-name glossary, section index, image credits, and research-method note.

Do not fix a chapter count or manuscript length before the inventory exists. A short, well-supported chapter is preferable to one padded with general history.

## Defining a section

Keep three concepts separate:

- **Stop:** a place from which the reader can identify one or more structures.
- **Physical section:** a locatable extent of surviving wall or moatwork.
- **Phase:** a construction or intervention represented in that section, with a documented or unresolved date.

A chapter's subject is a distinct physical section belonging to an identifiable phase. The phase may be distinguishable even when its calendar date is unknown. The same phase in physically separate remains can require separate chapters because the route encounters them separately.

### Boundary rules

- Record a visible change as an observation first. Do not turn it into a chronological boundary without supporting evidence.
- Where evidence distinguishes adjacent or overlapping phases, give each its own chapter and cross-reference the interface.
- Where two periods are established but their exact interface is unclear, retain both chapters. Map the uncertain boundary as a zone, not a false precise line.
- Where the number of phases is itself unresolved, keep a provisional composite inventory record. Do not manufacture period-specific chapters. A published account may explain the unresolved section without claiming a false division.
- Record small repairs within the relevant chapter unless evidence identifies them as a distinct period-specific section. Size alone does not decide whether a section gets a chapter.
- Record facing, core, base, and upper work separately where evidence permits. An old material reused in later work does not make the later construction old.

Assign stable section and phase identifiers independently of chapter numbers. Chapter order can change as research improves. Preserve split and merge history so that photographs, notes, and citations do not lose their referents.

## Inventory and field evidence

Make a full circuit inventory before commissioning the complete manuscript. A desk inventory is provisional until its limits are checked against current, dated evidence.

For each candidate section, record:

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Stable ID, feature type, verified name and variants |
| Position | Route order, bank or wall side, endpoints or extent, coordinate source and accuracy |
| Observation | What is visible, observer, date, viewpoint, photograph IDs |
| Extent | Boundary description, adjacent IDs, overlap or uncertain boundary zone |
| Phases | Distinguishable fabric, proposed attribution, supporting source, unresolved alternatives |
| Condition | Dated description of visible survival, damage, repairs, and obstructions |
| Evidence | Claim IDs, source IDs, exact locators, photographic or survey support |
| Access | Dated, verified viewing position; inaccessible or unexamined areas |
| Rights | Image creator, permissions, license, required credit |
| Status | Candidate, evidence reviewed, chapter-ready, or unresolved |

Photograph the overall setting, both ends, visible faces, junctions, and useful construction details. Keep original files and record camera direction. Show scale only where it can be obtained safely and accurately. Never climb, enter the moat, disturb masonry, or take samples for this project.

Record moatworks independently from nearby wall sections. Proximity is not evidence of the same construction date. Water level or vegetation may hide masonry; describe what was observable on the survey date without extrapolating below it.

A human observer's dated record can support a claim about what was seen. An AI agent must never claim to have visited, measured, or photographed a site. Remote images must retain their actual date and source; retrieval date is not observation date.

## Research and endnote standard

### Every fact has an evidence path

Each factual claim must lead through an endnote to inspected evidence that supports that exact claim. A bibliography alone is insufficient. A citation to a source about the same gate is insufficient if the source says nothing about the specific masonry or date asserted.

Maintain a claim ledger alongside the manuscript:

- Claim ID and proposed wording.
- Section or phase ID.
- Source ID and exact page, figure, archival identifier, or web heading and passage.
- Supporting excerpt or a precise evidence description.
- Original-language wording where translation matters.
- Translation and its review status.
- Source language, paired-language source where available, and whether the sources are independent.
- Evidence class: direct observation, documentary statement, scholarly interpretation, or unresolved.
- Conflicting evidence and limits of the source's scope.
- Review result: supported, needs narrower wording, unresolved, or rejected.
- Manuscript location and stable endnote identifier.

Only supported claims enter the factual narrative. A cited scholarly interpretation is not automatically established fact: attribute it as that scholar's interpretation and retain its uncertainty. If the evidence is too weak even for a useful attributed discussion, leave it out. Model inference is a research lead, not publishable historical evidence.

### Endnote form

Use a superscript note marker immediately after the factual statement it supports. Split a sentence when its clauses require different evidence. A note may support multiple closely related claims only when it makes the mapping clear. Never leave the reader guessing whether one note supports a whole paragraph.

Each note supplies enough information to recover and inspect the evidence:

- Author or responsible institution, title, and publication date where available.
- Exact page, plate, figure, catalog reference, or other locator.
- URL and access date for online material; an archival link where available.
- A short explanation where the source establishes only a date range, attribution, or limited observation.
- Photograph or field-record ID, observer or creator, observation date, and accessible repository reference for observational claims.

Missing source metadata stays explicitly missing; never invent a date, author, title, page number, quotation, or DOI. Preserve permitted research copies and record retrieval details. If evidence cannot be shared publicly, give its repository and access conditions without reproducing restricted content.

Factual captions, map labels, measurements, timelines, and tables need notes too. A map can use keyed feature IDs and an adjacent note list; it must still be possible to identify the source of each factual assertion. Illustration credit and factual support are separate obligations.

Endnotes must appear at the back of every edition, grouped by chapter. Electronic editions must link from claim to note and back. Do not substitute page footnotes, bare hyperlinks, or a bibliography for the requested endnotes.

### Footnotes have a different purpose

**Endnotes hold references. Footnotes hold asides, explanations, and supplementary detail.** Preserve that distinction in the source and in every edition. Do not send all notes through one undifferentiated note system.

In PDF, explanatory footnotes belong at the foot of the relevant page. In reflowable EPUB and HTML, preserve their identity as explanatory footnotes through accessible linked notes or reader-supported popovers, with a readable fallback. Reference endnotes remain in the back-of-book reference section.

A factual assertion inside a footnote still requires a reference endnote. The publishing trial must cover a footnote with its own endnote reference. Footnotes are not a place for uncited speculation. Generate the two note sequences separately; their appearance and numbering belong to shared styles, not chapter text.

### Source priorities and safeguards

Seek archaeological and conservation reports, measured surveys, dated plans and photographs, archival records, and relevant peer-reviewed or specialist scholarship. Search Thai-language material as well as English. Initial institutions to investigate include Thailand's Fine Arts Department, Chiang Mai municipal records, local university libraries, and museum or archival collections. These are research leads, not a claim that a particular report has been located.

Use chronicle editions with attention to transmission, translation, and the difference between the date described and the date of the surviving account. Verify Thai names and calendar conversions; keep original dates alongside conversions in research records. Do not apply a blanket year-offset rule to all historical dates.

Tourism pages, signage, blogs, and search results may help locate evidence. They do not establish the date of surviving fabric merely by repeating a familiar founding story. Read the underlying work. Record which sources copy a common source; repetition is not independent corroboration.

A primary source can be mistaken, retrospective, or ambiguous. Assess its purpose and relation to the claim, not just its label. For central dating and reconstruction claims, seek independent corroboration. If only one credible source exists, say what it establishes and do not imply consensus.

AI output, search snippets, and uninspected citations are not sources. Verify OCR and quotations against the original page. Have consequential Thai translations checked by a competent human reader before publication.

### Cite across languages where possible

For each substantive historical claim, seek both Thai-language evidence and relevant English-, French-, or other-language evidence. Where suitable sources exist, cite both in the same reference endnote, with precise locators for each. Do not merely add one Thai title and one English title to a chapter bibliography.

Prefer the source closest to the evidence regardless of language. A source in a second language must genuinely support the claim; a superficial mention does not earn a citation. Do not invent balance when only one suitable source exists. Record the unsuccessful search in the research dossier and publish only what the available evidence supports.

Retain original titles and author names, with transliteration or translated titles to help readers find them. Identify the edition and translator. A translation of the same work improves access but is not independent corroboration. If language versions or sources disagree, record and resolve the difference where possible; otherwise attribute the disagreement rather than combining them into a false consensus.

Machine translation is a discovery aid and a draft, not final authority for a consequential historical claim. Apply competent human review to consequential translations from any language, not only Thai.

### Unknowns are valid results

Use precise limits rather than sweeping negatives:

- “We have not established the construction date of this section” is a statement about this investigation.
- “No records survive” asserts knowledge of every possible record and requires much stronger evidence.
- “It may date from…” must not be a way to smuggle an agent's guess into the book.

Where the book states a research limit, attach an endnote describing the reviewed evidence and the unresolved question. Do not convert “not found” into “never existed.” If competing accounts cannot be reconciled, explain the disagreement with notes or omit the disputed detail.

## Standard chapter template

Use the following organization for every section. Keep it brief where evidence is sparse; do not force content into an empty heading.

1. **Identify the section.** Verified name, stable ID, feature type, location, and supported phase attribution or explicit unknown date. Locator map and current photograph, both sourced.
2. **What to look at.** Visible extent, material, condition, and diagnostic details. Tie observations to dated photographs or field records. Separate description from interpretation.
3. **What is known about its history.** A concise narrative supported claim by claim. Distinguish the site's history, the standing fabric, and subsequent interventions.
4. **Where it meets other work.** Explain adjoining or overlapping sections, the evidence for separation, and any uncertain boundary. Link to their chapters.
5. **What remains unknown.** Only consequential questions and conflicts. State why a date or attribution cannot responsibly be given.
6. **Continue clockwise.** Identify the next section or explain that the next chapter concerns another phase at this same stop. Include factual access directions only when verified and noted.

Use the same endnote system throughout, with chapter-specific groups at the back. Do not repeat a generic city history in every chapter. Cross-reference shared context, but do not make a cross-reference substitute for evidence supporting a new claim.

## AI research and editorial workflow

The editorial owner controls the section register, terminology, route order, and final acceptance. Agents work from that shared record rather than inventing independent inventories.

For each section:

1. Assemble a research dossier: inspected sources, extracts, image evidence, claim ledger, conflicts, and missing information.
2. Review the proposed boundaries and phase attribution before drafting a chapter around them.
3. Draft only from supported claims. Keep endnotes attached during drafting; do not add plausible prose first and hunt for citations later.
4. Have a separate verification pass reopen the cited evidence and check each factual clause. A second agent's agreement is not corroboration.
5. Obtain appropriate human review of consequential translations, archaeological interpretation, and visual identification.
6. Edit for clarity without strengthening certainty or separating a claim from its note. Recheck changed factual wording against the evidence.

After the shared inventory and source conventions exist, research dossiers can be prepared in parallel. Shared interfaces and overlapping sections still need one editorial decision. Record model and research date internally, and disclose AI generation honestly in the published method note. Human review supplies accountability; it does not make an unsupported claim true.

## Single-source publishing approach

**Working recommendation: semantic Pandoc Markdown for the manuscript, with Typst as the first PDF-engine candidate.** Keep this provisional until the two-note-system publishing trial passes. Pandoc documents Markdown, HTML, EPUB, and Typst support, including Typst as a PDF engine.[1][2]

Use existing publishing tools. Do not build a custom authoring application, CMS, or research database. Keep the section register and claim ledger in simple structured files. Keep source IDs, chapter IDs, figure IDs, and both types of note IDs stable.

### Typst assessment

Typst is a credible candidate for book typesetting. Its template system separates document content from formatting.[6] It deserves a trial for the PDF edition, including Thai text, maps, endnotes, and footnotes.

It is not yet the recommended master format for this project. The Typst HTML documentation inspected for this plan explicitly labels native HTML export experimental and says not to use it for production.[7] That matters because the website is a required edition, not an optional export.

Pandoc also lists Typst as an input format.[1] A Typst-authored manuscript could therefore be evaluated through a separate conversion path; the existence of that reader does not prove that arbitrary Typst functions, templates, or two distinct note systems survive conversion. Do not promise an EPUB path until a representative sample has passed it.

The proposed division is therefore Markdown content → Pandoc → HTML and EPUB, with Pandoc → Typst → PDF. This uses Typst where it can add value without making the website depend on experimental native HTML export. This is a documented recommendation, not an implemented or verified publishing pipeline.

### Chapters contain meaning, not visual formatting

The entire book must be restylable without editing chapter files. Chapter files contain prose, semantic headings, quotations, lists, figures, captions, source references, explanatory footnotes, and stable cross-references. They may name a semantic role such as “locator map” or “uncertain boundary”; they must not specify how that role looks.

Keep fonts, sizes, colors, page dimensions, margins, spacing, heading appearance, figure placement and sizing, note numbering, and navigation design in shared templates, styles, and build configuration. Keep chapter order and edition metadata outside individual chapters too.

Do not put inline CSS, raw layout HTML, visual Typst commands, manual page breaks, manually numbered notes or figures, or output-specific conditionals in chapter files. A genuine semantic exception gets a named role whose rendering is defined centrally, not a local formatting patch.

The acceptance test is concrete: produce two visibly different book designs by changing only shared presentation files. Chapter files remain byte-for-byte unchanged, and both designs retain the same content, references, note distinction, and navigation.

Select an existing publishing extension or template that demonstrably supports this distinction. If none does, review the need for a small shared conversion layer before adopting it; do not silently merge the note types or embed rendering instructions in chapters. Never maintain a separately edited PDF or Kindle manuscript.

Publish the HTML edition on its own Cloudflare Pages project. Build static files in CI and upload the generated directory using Pages Direct Upload. Use pull-request preview deployments for review before publishing an approved release. Keep the book's deployment configuration and credentials independent of other sites.

The website should provide:

- A short explanation that the EPUB can be sent through Amazon's Send to Kindle service. Do not label a raw EPUB as a native Kindle file or imply direct USB compatibility without verification.[8]
- A contents page, clockwise previous/next links, section index, endnotes, bibliography, and image credits.
- A source-repository link, license information, edition identifier, research cutoff, and a corrections link.
- Download sizes and edition identifiers so readers know what they are obtaining.

One EPUB can serve Kindle delivery and other EPUB readers; it need not be two different downloads. Useful additional downloads are an offline HTML archive and the editable source archive. Defer additional proprietary formats unless a reader need is established. Offline editions carry no analytics.

Build the website and downloads from the same approved source revision. Use versioned artifact names and ensure the landing page links to that edition, not to a mixture of old and new files. The publishing build must not query the live site or live research sources to reconstruct book content.

Cloudflare Pages currently limits a single site asset to 25 MiB.[9] Measure the illustrated PDF and EPUB during the trial. Keep them within that limit without making maps unreadable; if necessary, serve versioned downloads from separate object storage while keeping the HTML on Pages. Decide that from measured sizes, not in advance.

Markdown changes are content changes for this project. Do not exclude manuscript files from build change detection. Changes to chapters, notes, bibliography, images, or presentation must rebuild the affected editions.

### Paid Kindle edition

Make the book regardless of whether it can be sold. Also plan to offer a paid Kindle edition on Amazon: the owner would buy such a book and wants that option available to other readers. Amazon acceptance and revenue are not prerequisites for completing or publishing the book.

Free online reading and commercial editions can coexist. For example, Al Sweigart's *Automate the Boring Stuff with Python* provides its full text free online and links to Amazon purchases.[14] This is evidence of the distribution model, not a guarantee that Amazon will accept this book or apply the same terms.

Use ordinary Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP), not KDP Select. Ordinary KDP's distribution license is nonexclusive. Select's additional restriction covers the book, or a substantially similar book, in digital format; withholding a compiled EPUB does not make a public full manuscript exclusive.[15]

Before submission, resolve the publishing-rights declaration for this predominantly AI-written work with KDP, disclose AI generation, and clear the rights to all included material. Do not claim copyright over the entire manuscript merely to fit a submission option.

Amazon reserves retail pricing discretion and has rules for matching free editions.[15, section 5.3.4][16, section 1.D] A public source repository with EPUB build instructions has no express price-matching exemption in the inspected terms. Whether to publish a ready-made EPUB download remains open; do not withhold it solely on an assumed pricing advantage. Free HTML and PDF editions must also be included in any question put to KDP about the distribution arrangement.

If accepted, add a clearly labeled purchase link to the website alongside its free reading and download options. Keep store preparation separate from the editorial acceptance gates, so a store rejection or unfavorable terms cannot prevent the book from existing.

### Umami visibility

Include Umami on the published HTML edition through the shared site template, never through individual chapter files. Load the vendor-hosted tracker and allow both its script origin and its event destination in the Content Security Policy; these may be different origins.

Give the book its own Umami website identity. Do not reuse another site's website ID or mix its readership into the book's figures. Keep credentials out of the public source; the public tracker configuration is not an administrative credential.

Measure page views and format-specific download-link clicks. A click is not proof of a completed download or a read book. Keep analytics out of PDF, EPUB, offline HTML, local builds, and review previews. Umami documents domain restrictions and controls for excluding query strings and URL fragments.[10] Restrict collection to the publication domain and avoid collecting reader-entered data or treating endnote navigation as a new chapter view.

Respect blockers; do not proxy the tracker to evade them. The book, notes, and downloads must work with analytics blocked or JavaScript disabled. Publish a concise analytics notice. Do not add a second analytics beacon without a separate decision to do so.

Verify the integration in an isolated preview with a test collector: cover a chapter page, the download page, a download click, and blocked analytics. Do not send test traffic into the publication's readership data or inspect a production system for this planning task.

## Copyright, rights, and disclosure

Copyright uncertainty does not block research or writing. It does require accurate claims about rights.

The intended process involves substantial research but no original human writing. Under the U.S. Copyright Office's analysis, research effort, expense, fact-checking, and prompting do not by themselves establish copyright in AI-written prose. U.S. law requires original human expression, not “sweat of the brow.” Historical facts remain unprotected regardless of how difficult they were to discover.[12, pp. 7–10]

No original human writing does not necessarily mean no protectable element anywhere in the finished book. A human's sufficiently creative selection, coordination, or arrangement of material can qualify, as can original human photographs, illustrations, or expressive revisions if present. That protection extends to those contributions, not to the underlying AI prose or historical facts.[12, pp. 24–27] Do not assume that the proposed clockwise route or a standardized chapter template meets this threshold. Assess the actual finished contribution rather than manufacturing nominal human involvement to obtain a copyright claim.

This is a U.S. analysis, not a worldwide determination. For example, UK legislation has a specific authorship rule for computer-generated works; its application and other requirements need separate analysis.[13] The position in Thailand has not been established by this plan. Obtain jurisdiction-specific legal advice before relying on exclusivity, asserting ownership of the entire book, or choosing final licensing terms.

Track human-authored contributions and third-party rights separately from AI-generated text. Photographs, maps, quoted passages, translations, and archival reproductions need their own rights assessment. A freely accessible image is not automatically free to republish. A license for our contributions cannot grant rights in somebody else's material.

Leave the licensing decision open while assessing what rights, if any, attach to the actual contributions in the relevant jurisdictions. “Freely available,” “public source,” and “Creative Commons licensed” are not interchangeable. Any store arrangement must remain compatible with the intended public repository, website, downloads, and whatever reuse terms the owner ultimately selects.

KDP currently requires disclosure of AI-generated text, images, and translations, including generated content substantially edited afterwards.[5] Plan to make that disclosure if using KDP. Recheck the current requirements at publication. Include a reader-facing account of the research method and AI use without claiming human authorship that did not occur.

## Work sequence and acceptance gates

### 1. Establish the research foundation

Locate authoritative source collections, create the source register, and make a provisional full-circuit inventory. Identify missing coverage and fieldwork needs. Keep the chapter count open.

Acceptance: candidate remains can be located; wall and moatworks are separate records; observed boundaries are not presented as established periods; every source status is explicit; Thai and non-Thai searches and source dependencies are recorded.

### 2. Prove the method near the starting point

Research the North Gate opening group and the next identifiable section clockwise. Include a mixed-phase interface if one is evidenced. Include a moatwork sample if surviving masonry can be identified; otherwise record the evidence gap rather than inventing one.

Acceptance: the chapter template works for the available evidence; every factual statement has a verified endnote; unsupported dates remain unknown; section and phase boundaries are reviewable.

### 3. Prove the publishing format

Render the sample material as PDF, EPUB, and HTML before expanding the manuscript. Exercise a photograph, locator map, Thai name, cross-reference, paired-language reference endnote, explanatory footnote, and a reference endnote cited from inside that footnote. Use real sourced material, not invented history to fill a demonstration.

Acceptance: validate EPUB structure and inspect it in Kindle Previewer; open the PDF and HTML; verify both note systems, return links, reading order, font rendering, image legibility, and offline usability. Render two distinct designs without changing chapter files. Exercise the HTML download links against the generated artifacts, check asset sizes, and verify Umami behavior with an isolated test collector. Record unavailable device coverage rather than claiming universal compatibility.

### 4. Complete the circuit

Finish the inventory and section dossiers, then draft chapters in route order. Seek specialist review where the evidence calls for it. Revise shared boundaries centrally.

Acceptance: every inventoried extant section has a chapter or an explicit unresolved treatment; every distinct evidenced period-specific section has its own chapter; survey gaps are disclosed. No section is silently omitted because it is less picturesque or less well documented.

### 5. Audit and publish

Audit every factual claim against its endnote and underlying evidence. Check captions and maps as strictly as prose. Resolve rights, book metadata, accessibility, and output checks. Publish editions from the same approved source revision, with a survey cutoff, edition date, and corrections route.

Acceptance: no unsupported factual claim remains; unresolved history is marked or omitted; all editions carry the same factual content and distinct, usable note systems; the public source can reproduce them; download links identify matching editions; permissions, analytics notice, and required disclosures are complete.

- Copyright position in relevant jurisdictions and the choice between an open-content license and free access without a broad reuse license.
- Publication domain, author or editor credit, and publisher identity.
- Kindle price, KDP publishing-rights classification, and whether to provide a ready-made EPUB download in addition to buildable source and a proposed paid store edition.
- Final image density and length, determined by the evidence rather than a page target.

The next research deliverable is a **provisional route inventory and source register**, not a polished opening chapter. Establish what is present and what evidence can support it before writing its history.
